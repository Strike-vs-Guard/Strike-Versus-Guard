On 13 November we flew a Starling by hand for the first time, with a gamepad, on the same software
that flies the drones on their own. The fence and the safety filter stay on while a person is on the
sticks, so a hand-flown drone is checked the same way as an autonomous one.

First we flew one drone alone. We turned it on the spot, flew it into the fence wall three times at
up to 0.66 m/s, and let go of the stick at about 0.5 m/s. The fence stopped it 3 cm past the wall at
most. After each release it coasted 28 cm or less and was still within 1.6 s.

Then we put the second Starling up to hover, raised the filter's keep-out radius from 0.55 m to 0.9 m
from the ground station while both were in the air, and flew the hand-flown drone straight at the
hovering one three times. The closest the two came was 1.76 m, against a pass line of 1.4 m. The
filter does not steer the hand-flown drone; it holds it off and eases the hovering one aside, by up
to 0.72 m. Both held their height within 6 cm.
